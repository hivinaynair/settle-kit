## Product behavior (D10–D19)

| ID | Decision | Why | Rejected | Date | Supersedes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D10 | Three ways to pay: (1) send to an address or scan a QR code (EIP-681) from any wallet or exchange; (2) connect a browser wallet (EIP-6963) and pay in USDC with one click; (3) connect a browser wallet and pay with any token it holds, swapped and bridged to USDC by Relay | (1) reaches exchanges and phone wallets; (2) locks the amount and network; (3) matches Fun.xyz for payers without USDC | Wallet only; address only | 2026-10-14 | |
| D11 | Address and QR payments accept a fixed list of proven routes: USDC on Base and Arbitrum, USDT on Arbitrum, ETH on Base and Arbitrum. A route is added only after a recorded mainnet payment. Wallet payments may use any token Relay can quote above a minimum value | An address payment can't be stopped if it's wrong, so each route needs a known minimum, fee and refund behavior; a failed wallet quote charges nothing. Ethereum needs a 25 USDC minimum, $0.50–5 sweep gas and a slow `safe` block; Tron is not EVM; USDT on Base is a bridged copy exchanges rarely send | Every token Relay supports on the address path; USDC-only; USDT on Base; Ethereum and Tron (X8) | 2026-10-15 | |
| D13 | The payer bears fees: route fees on Relay, the flat sweep fee (D39) and the CCTP fee on own rails. The widget shows them ("you'll receive about X") before sending; credit is the verified arrival amount | Merchants receive what they priced; fees are visible up front | The merchant absorbs fees | 2026-10-15 | |
| D14 | The host is told by signed webhook events: `order.paid`, `order.needs_attention`, `deposit.credited` | Merchants hear about problems, not only successes; three events stay small | A settlement-only callback; a general event bus | 2026-10-14 | |
| D15 | A checkout payment that arrives after the order expired still pays the order automatically, if the order wasn't cancelled and the amount meets the rule for its lane (exact on Relay, D24; at least the price on CCTP Fast, D25). `order.paid` then carries `late: true` so the host can tell | The buyer paid in full and shouldn't wait on a person; the host still sees it was late | Holding every late payment in `needs_attention` for an operator; a config switch between the two | 2026-10-14 | |
| D17 | A payment goes to `needs_attention` immediately when something is clearly wrong (an amount that breaks its lane's rule, a wrong recipient or token, a payment to a cancelled order, a Relay refund or failure), and after 15 minutes from first being seen without a verified outcome (`delayed`). The widget says "taking longer than usual; your payment is safe" at 2 minutes. Attention cases are re-checked every 15 minutes for 7 days, then daily; only a verified result on Base (D21) moves one out. An order with no payment seen simply expires | Normal payments credit in under 2 minutes, so 15 minutes flags real problems without false alarms; the 7-day taper protects Relay's rate limit | 30 minutes (archived plan); 5 minutes; re-checking every 15 minutes forever | 2026-10-14 | |
| D18 | A deposit shows "Received" with its amount as soon as our own read finds the transfer at the `latest` block; it is recorded and credited only at the `safe` block (D21) | Feels instant (~2 s instead of ~30 s) while money still moves only on proof, as for checkout's "Paid" | Showing nothing until `safe`; recording at `latest` | 2026-10-15 | |

## Money rules (D20–D29)

| ID | Decision | Why | Rejected | Date | Supersedes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D20 | The destination is always USDC on Base. Every order and deposit address stores its destination (chain ID and token contract) next to its recipient, and every check reads it from there; no code assumes Base. Tokens are identified by chain ID and contract address, never by symbol | One asset keeps verification exact and the host's accounting simple; a stored destination makes USDC on another chain a config entry plus a live proof later | USDC on Base, Arbitrum and Ethereum in v1.0 (up to 21 route proofs, minutes-long waits on Ethereum); any coin as destination; matching by symbol | 2026-10-14 | |
| D21 | Credit only what we verified ourselves on Base at the `safe` block (~30 s). The buyer sees "Paid" as soon as our own check of the Base transaction succeeds at the `latest` block (~2–5 s). Relay's status may drive progress messages ("payment received") but never marks a payment paid and never authorizes credit; elapsed time never authorizes credit or a "lost" verdict | Feels instant to the buyer while money moves only on proof; reading the Base receipt costs one call; a rollback after "Paid" shows in needs_attention with nothing credited. S3: `safe` is ~30 s on Base | Crediting on Relay's status; a per-host "credit at latest" option (two money paths for little gain); a fixed confirmation count; waiting for `finalized` (~16 min) | 2026-10-14 | |
| D22 | Amounts are base units: `bigint` in code, `numeric` in Postgres, decimal strings in JSON. Never floats | Floats lose cents | Floats; decimal libraries in the hot path | 2026-10-13 | |
| D23 | Credit exactly once: unique keys per provider fill, per on-chain transfer `(chain ID, tx hash, log index)` and per paid order | Duplicate webhooks, retries and two workers are normal; the database must refuse the second credit | Application-level checks only | 2026-10-14 | |
| D24 | Checkout on the Relay lane uses a strict `EXACT_OUTPUT` deposit address: the order is paid only when the treasury receives exactly the order amount. Less is refunded by Relay; more fills the exact amount and Relay refunds the excess | A transfer can't be stopped from carrying the wrong amount, so the outcome is made exact | Open addresses with tolerance for checkout | 2026-10-14 | |
| D25 | On the CCTP Fast path the fee comes out of the minted USDC, so checkout accepts at least the order amount and records any surplus | CCTP Fast can't deliver an exact amount; Standard is exact but takes 15–19 minutes | CCTP Standard for checkout; rejecting every non-exact amount | 2026-10-14 | |
| D26 | Treasury and refund addresses come from server config only. An order's or address's recipient is stored when it is created, and verification checks against the stored value, not current settings | A changed setting must not redirect or reject money already in flight | Client-supplied addresses; checking current config | 2026-10-14 | |
| D27 | Settle Kit never holds keys that move user funds and never repays payers itself. Relay refunds go to the host's configured refund address; the host repays through its own support process | Exchange senders can't receive a refund at the sending address; automatic transfers to unverified destinations lose money | Refund to the sender automatically | 2026-10-13 | |
| D28 | Limits: checkout price at least 0.50 USDC; deposit minimum 2 USDC on Base and Arbitrum. Below the minimum on own rails, the deposit waits at the address and is swept and credited once the address's balance reaches it; on Relay, Relay's own minimum applies and a refund becomes `needs_attention` (D17). No displayed maximum by default; a host may set one, shown as guidance only. No cap on the amount a sweep moves | S9: Relay's fee is ~flat (0.025–0.034 USDC), and the sweep fee is fixed, so small amounts lose too much below these; a transfer's size can't be enforced; a per-sweep cap is bypassed by calling repeatedly | One minimum for every chain; refunding small deposits (costs more gas than they're worth); a 1,000 USDC per-sweep contract cap | 2026-10-15 | |
| D29 | An own-rails checkout is paid when the USDC verified as arriving at its stored recipient on Base (D21), after the sweep fee and the CCTP fee, totals at least the price. The widget asks for the price plus the maximum fees ("Send 25.06 USDC"), a bound we can compute because the sweep fee is fixed (D39) and CCTP takes a maximum fee, so the recipient receives at least the price; anything above is recorded as surplus (as D25). A short total waits for a top-up to the same address; at 15 minutes it becomes `needs_attention: underpaid` (D17), and a later top-up still completes it | Own rails have no strict address, and exchanges often deduct their withdrawal fee from the amount; counting gross arrivals at the deposit address would mark orders paid while the merchant received less than the price | Exact amount only; judging each transfer on its own; counting gross arrivals at the deposit address; the merchant absorbing fees | 2026-10-15 | |
| D121 | Deposits are credited per sweep, attributed by chain order: once a sweep transaction is confirmed, every USDC transfer into the address that came before it on that chain belongs to that sweep, recorded if not yet. Its verified arrival on Base is one credit covering those transfers, recorded only when each is at or below `safe` and their sum matches what the sweep moved (else `needs_attention`); `deposit.credited` is sent once per credit with the verified net amount and the transfers it covers. A transfer belongs to at most one credit | One sweep makes one arrival, so one arrival pays for every deposit it moved; `sweep()` moves the whole live balance, so a transfer landing between recording and the sweep must be linked too | Splitting one arrival across separate credits; forbidding a second deposit before the first is swept; linking only deposits recorded when the sweep was sent; a `sweep(amount)` snapshot (a caller-chosen input to a function anyone can call) | 2026-10-15 | |

## Architecture (D30–D39)

| ID | Decision | Why | Rejected | Date | Supersedes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D30 | Postgres with Drizzle in the host's own database, shipped with numbered SQL migrations that are never edited once published | Unique constraints, conditional updates and transactions are what make credit-once safe | Our own hosted database; a pluggable database layer | 2026-10-13 | |
| D31 | One schema covers orders and deposit addresses from the first migration | With one release, design the data once instead of reshaping it halfway | Checkout tables first, deposits added later | 2026-10-14 | |
| D32 | Payments move through a state machine with final states and compare-and-set transitions (update only if still in the expected state) | Two workers can't both move the same payment; final states never change | Free-form status updates | 2026-10-14 | |
| D33 | Own-rails contracts (Foundry, MIT, written from scratch, not copied from Daimo's GPL code). Each account (or, in slice 2, each order) gets a CREATE2 deposit address, known before deployment and created by the shared factory (D134) with a unique salt (D135). Each address stores, fixed at creation: its recipient (the treasury), its refund address, its sweep minimum (D28) and its sweep fee (D39). `sweep()` pays the fee to the gas wallet and sends the rest of the USDC to the recipient (Base forwards directly; Arbitrum goes through CCTP V2 Fast with the Forwarding Service); anyone may call it at or above the minimum, and below it only the host's gas wallet may, so a short payment is never stuck. `rescue(token)`, callable by anyone, sends any other token or native ETH only to the refund address, never USDC. There is no refund function: our code, not the contract, decides what a payment means (late: D15; attention cases: D17), and money the host must return goes through its refund process (D27) | No trusted operator role: every function sends only to destinations fixed at creation; the address can be shown before any gas is spent; wrong tokens are recoverable; a contract refund could only go to the host, which `sweep()` already does; CCTP needs no account or KYC; CCTP V1 pauses on 2026-12-01 | Relay for USDC too; CCTP V1; a privileged sweeper role; no rescue (wrong tokens stuck forever); a contract refund to the sender or the refund address; no minimum in the contract | 2026-10-15 | |
| D34 | Our own indexer per chain reads USDC `Transfer` logs up to the `safe` block, saves its position in the same transaction as the transfers, and stores each transfer once by `(chain ID, tx hash, log index)` | A chain never notifies anyone; our own addresses have no Relay to ask; reading only to `safe` avoids rollback logic | Ponder or another indexing framework; reading to the chain head | 2026-10-14 | |
| D35 | Payments are found by push (Relay webhooks, the indexer), with discovery reconciliation on a schedule as the safety net, and reconciliation of the books: every credit is matched to a verified arrival at its stored recipient on Base, and every arrival at the treasury is matched to a credit | Webhooks get lost; long-lived deposit addresses can't each be polled every 10 seconds; reconciliation proves production matches the books | Polling every address; trusting webhooks alone | 2026-10-15 | |
| D36 | Per-payment processes run as DBOS Transact workflows (MIT; state in the host's own Postgres, no other service): Relay settlement, the own-rails sweep (one at a time per address), and webhook delivery. Scanning work (the indexer, discovery reconciliation, reconciliation of the books) runs on a schedule. Settle Kit pins DBOS's application version to its own release, never the code hash, and keeps workflow code compatible across releases (same steps in the same order; an incompatible change gets a new workflow name). Each process gets its own executor ID and records a heartbeat; the scheduled check resumes workflows left pending by a process whose heartbeat is older than 2 minutes. Every step with a side effect is safe to run twice. The database stays the source of truth (D32, D23) | S12: recovery in ~1 s after a crash during a sleep or inside a step, on Node and Bun, with nothing to run but Postgres; S12 also showed the hash version strands runs on redeploy, a shared executor ID can run a step twice, and an orphaned run is rescued with `resumeWorkflow` | Vercel Workflow SDK (ties hosts to its framework adapters; S10: ~14 min recovery self-hosted, unauthenticated flow route); Trigger.dev, Hatchet, Temporal (a separate service every host must run); Inngest server (SSPL); Restate server (BSL); our own job runner (DBOS already does it) | 2026-10-15 | |
| D37 | Settle Kit runs in any Node 20+ or Bun server process (Express, Fastify, Hono, Next.js, plain Node or Bun) or on serverless platforms, with any Postgres. No framework plugin or build step is required | Self-hosting is the point of the project, and the people who self-host use many stacks; S12 showed DBOS needs only a process and Postgres | Requiring a framework supported by the Workflow SDK (the earlier plan); Express and plain Node out of scope | 2026-10-15 | |
| D38 | Settle Kit chooses the lane per coin when it issues an address (USDC → own rails; USDT, ETH and wallet any-token payments → Relay), stores it, and never changes it. There is no automatic fallback to Relay; a host may force a lane per coin (D46). If CCTP fails after a permanent deposit address was issued, the funds wait at the contract and the sweep retries; the user is never moved to a new address | The address is the lane, and a payer may already have sent to it; issuing an own-rails address is a local calculation that does not fail, so a fallback would be code that never runs | Switching lanes after the address is shown; the buyer choosing; Relay for everything; automatic fallback at issue time | 2026-10-15 | |
| D39 | Each deposit address stores a flat sweep fee in USDC, fixed at creation (default 0.01 USDC on Base, 0.05 USDC on Arbitrum; a host may set its own). Every sweep pays exactly that fee to the host's gas-wallet address stored in the contract, and the rest goes to the recipient. The gas wallet (key in server config, used only to deploy addresses and call `sweep()`) pays the real gas in ETH, and the host absorbs the difference when gas prices move. Wallet payments skip the sweep: the buyer signs one `permit` (EIP-2612) transaction that forwards straight to the treasury | Someone must sign the second transaction and only the host can; a flat fee needs no ETH price (X3), is exact to show and to reconcile, and is still borne by the buyer (D13); a stolen gas key can't move user funds (D27), and the fixed fee and recipient stop a stranger's `sweep()` from taking more | Refunding measured gas in USDC (needs a price source); a USDC paymaster (ERC-4337 machinery); the host paying all gas; reimbursing whoever calls `sweep()` | 2026-10-15 | |
| D131 | Scheduled work runs in one of two ways, both supported: on a long-running server, `settle.start()` registers DBOS schedules that scan the chains every 10 s and run the rest of the scheduled check every minute; on serverless, a cron calls a protected `POST` route every minute (Vercel Pro's smallest interval, or any external cron). Speed also comes from targeted scans: while a `<Deposit>` widget is open, its status checks scan that one address. An RPC provider's address-activity webhook may later trigger the same targeted scan; it is never proof (D21), and it is not in slice 1 | Credit waits for `safe` (~30 s) anyway, so a 10 s or 1 min scan adds little, and a watching user sees ~30–40 s either way; serverless hosts have no long-running process; fewer RPC calls keep the free tier | Only Vercel Cron (ties hosts to Vercel); an always-on worker as the only option; a never-ending workflow loop | 2026-10-15 | |
| D134 | One shared deposit-address factory per chain, deployed once by Settle Kit at the same address on Base and Arbitrum; a business may set its own factory address, deployed from the same code | Every per-address setting is fixed inside the address, and the factory holds no money and has no owner, so sharing gives no business power over another; setup loses a deploy step | Every business deploying its own factory (extra step, no safety gain) | 2026-10-15 | |
| D135 | Each deposit address's CREATE2 salt is derived from the business's address namespace (32 random bytes generated once at setup and kept in config) and the account ID, so no two accounts, and no two businesses on the shared factory, get the same address; stored addresses are always read back, never re-derived | With a shared factory and a common treasury recipient, the other inputs are identical across accounts, so the address must be made unique on purpose (Codex on #15) | Salting with the account ID alone (collides across businesses); a database-generated salt (lost with the database) | 2026-10-15 | |
