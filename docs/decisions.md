## Product behavior (D10–D19)

| ID | Decision | Why | Rejected | Date | Supersedes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D10 | Three ways to pay: (1) send to an address or scan a QR code (EIP-681) from any wallet or exchange; (2) connect a browser wallet (EIP-6963) and pay in USDC with one click; (3) connect a browser wallet and pay with any token it holds, swapped and bridged to USDC by Relay | (1) reaches exchanges and phone wallets; (2) locks the amount and network; (3) matches Fun.xyz for payers without USDC | Wallet only; address only | 2026-10-14 | |
| D11 | Address and QR payments accept a fixed list of proven routes: USDC on Base and Arbitrum, USDT on Arbitrum, ETH on Base and Arbitrum. A route is added only after a recorded mainnet payment. Wallet payments may use any token Relay can quote above a minimum value | An address payment can't be stopped if it's wrong, so each route needs a known minimum, fee and refund behavior; a failed wallet quote charges nothing. Ethereum needs a 25 USDC minimum, $0.50–5 sweep gas and a slow `safe` block; Tron is not EVM; USDT on Base is a bridged copy exchanges rarely send | Every token Relay supports on the address path; USDC-only; USDT on Base; Ethereum and Tron (X8) | 2026-10-15 | |
| D13 | The payer bears fees: route fees on Relay, the flat sweep fee (D39) and the CCTP fee on own rails. The widget shows them ("you'll receive about X") before sending; credit is the verified arrival amount | Merchants receive what they priced; fees are visible up front | The merchant absorbs fees | 2026-10-15 | |
| D14 | The host is told by signed webhook events: `order.paid`, `order.needs_attention`, `deposit.credited` and `deposit.needs_attention`. Slice 1 sends the two deposit events through the durable event path (D230). Each transition into a deposit attention episode creates one immutable attention event with its account, reason, processing stage and known transaction identities; retries retain its identity and payload | A business needs to hear about stalled deposits as well as successful settlements | Deposit success notifications only; a general event bus | 2026-10-15 | |
| D15 | A checkout payment that arrives after the order expired still pays the order automatically, if the order wasn't cancelled and the amount meets the rule for its lane (exact on Relay, D24; at least the price on CCTP Fast, D25). `order.paid` then carries `late: true` so the host can tell | The buyer paid in full and shouldn't wait on a person; the host still sees it was late | Holding every late payment in `needs_attention` for an operator; a config switch between the two | 2026-10-14 | |
| D17 | A payment enters `needs_attention` when a verified check finds an invalid outcome; a deposit also enters it for an over-limit balance, a failed forwarding operation or an unaffordable route fee. A deposit below its stored minimum, with no sweep pending, stays `awaiting_minimum` without a delay timeout. Its processing-delay timer starts once its safe, unassigned funds first meet the minimum; it is not reset by retries, top-ups or workflow restarts. At 2 minutes without settlement the widget says "Taking longer than usual; checking your deposit"; at 15 minutes it enters `needs_attention: delayed` if no other attention reason is already recorded. A provisional observation disappearing from the canonical chain gets "Confirmation changed; checking" (D18). Attention cases are checked every 15 minutes for 7 days from entry, then daily. Over-limit recovery remains visible as `needs_attention: recovered_to_refund_address`; it is not a Base credit or proof of repayment (D27). Other attention cases leave only after all settlement checks pass (D21, D121). A checkout with no payment seen expires; its underpayment timer remains D29 | Intentional minimum accumulation is not a failed transfer, and a retry must not erase elapsed waiting time | Timing out sub-minimum deposits; resetting the delay timer on every retry; declaring funds safe or lost from elapsed time alone | 2026-10-15 | |
| D18 | The widget shows "Received" with a provisional amount when our own read finds a transfer at `latest`. Provisional observations are separate from deposit records and never authorize money movement or credit. A canonical replacement block that excludes the observed transfer retracts that provisional amount and shows "Confirmation changed; checking". Deposit records require source-chain `safe` (D34). Internal settlement credit requires the corresponding Base arrival at `safe` (D21); while the host has not acknowledged committed account processing, the widget says "Received by the business; account update pending". It shows the account as credited only after that acknowledgment (D124). The minimal `useDeposits` hook exposes the same provisional, minimum, processing, attention, settlement and delivery states and uses the same targeted scans as `<Deposit>` | Show observed progress without promising a business balance update that has not happened | Treating a latest observation as a deposit record; showing account credited before host acknowledgment; keeping orphaned provisional amounts | 2026-10-15 | |

## Money rules (D20–D29)

| ID | Decision | Why | Rejected | Date | Supersedes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D20 | The destination is always USDC on Base. Every order and deposit address stores its destination (chain ID and token contract) next to its recipient, and every check reads it from there; no code assumes Base. Tokens are identified by chain ID and contract address, never by symbol | One asset keeps verification exact and the host's accounting simple; a stored destination makes USDC on another chain a config entry plus a live proof later | USDC on Base, Arbitrum and Ethereum in v1.0 (up to 21 route proofs, minutes-long waits on Ethereum); any coin as destination; matching by symbol | 2026-10-14 | |
| D21 | Create an internal credit only for a canonical, independently verified USDC arrival at the stored recipient on Base at or below the captured `safe` block, uniquely claimed by that credit and in its verified net amount. `safe` is the block tag, never a fixed number of seconds or confirmations. The buyer may see checkout "Paid" after our Base check at `latest`; deposit account-credit wording is D18. Provider statuses drive progress only and never authorize credit; elapsed time never authorizes credit or a lost verdict. Retain receipt block hashes and transfer identities so reconciliation can detect a subsequent loss of canonical evidence (D125) | Credit on chain evidence and identify the evidence later if the chain reorganizes | Crediting provider status; a credit-at-latest option; a fixed confirmation count; treating a sample safe-block delay as a guarantee | 2026-10-15 | |
| D22 | Amounts are base units: `bigint` in code, `numeric` in Postgres, decimal strings in JSON. Never floats | Floats lose cents | Floats; decimal libraries in the hot path | 2026-10-13 | |
| D23 | Credit exactly once: database uniqueness per provider fill, source transfer `(chain ID, tx hash, log index)`, on-chain sweep `(chain ID, tx hash, sweep-event log index)`, verified destination transfer `(chain ID, tx hash, log index)` and paid order. Claims, source memberships, the credit and its outgoing event are committed atomically (D230). Host-account deduplication is a separate atomic obligation (D124) | Concurrent scans, external sweeps, workflow retries and webhook redelivery must converge on the same records | Application-level checks only; assuming internal credit uniqueness also deduplicates the host balance | 2026-10-15 | |
| D24 | Checkout on the Relay lane uses a strict `EXACT_OUTPUT` deposit address: the order is paid only when the treasury receives exactly the order amount. Less is refunded by Relay; more fills the exact amount and Relay refunds the excess | A transfer can't be stopped from carrying the wrong amount, so the outcome is made exact | Open addresses with tolerance for checkout | 2026-10-14 | |
| D25 | On the CCTP Fast path the fee comes out of the minted USDC, so checkout accepts at least the order amount and records any surplus | CCTP Fast can't deliver an exact amount; Standard is exact but takes 15–19 minutes | CCTP Standard for checkout; rejecting every non-exact amount | 2026-10-14 | |
| D26 | Treasury and refund addresses come from server config only. An order's or address's recipient is stored when it is created, and verification checks against the stored value, not current settings | A changed setting must not redirect or reject money already in flight | Client-supplied addresses; checking current config | 2026-10-14 | |
| D27 | The SDK holds only the host gas-wallet keys needed to pay gas and invoke fixed-destination contract functions; it does not hold treasury or payer keys and cannot choose a new destination during a sweep or recovery. Relay refunds and own-rails over-limit recoveries go only to the host refund address stored for that payment or address. The host repays payers through its own support process after verifying where repayment belongs; source-chain recovery is not Base settlement or evidence that the payer was repaid | Exchange withdrawal senders may be shared wallets, and a recovery to the host is only the start of repayment | Repaying a transaction sender automatically; treating the host refund receipt as payer repayment | 2026-10-15 | |
| D28 | Checkout price is at least 0.50 USDC; reusable deposit minimum is 2 USDC on Base and Arbitrum. Every caller, including the gas wallet, must meet the stored minimum to sweep; smaller deposits wait for top-up. Automatic Arbitrum processing also requires a gross address balance at most the address's stored maximum, fixed at creation (**default 1,000 USDC, 1,000,000,000 base units**; a host may configure its own) and a net burn no greater than Circle's current on-chain per-message burn limit. These are checked again at contract execution. Exceeding either limit blocks the entire automatic sweep and enters `needs_attention: over_limit`; no partial burn or sweep fee is charged. `recoverOverLimit()` may instead move the entire source-chain USDC balance to the stored host refund address without a sweep fee (D33); recovery is manual and produces no Base credit. Base has no protocol processing maximum; a host-displayed guidance maximum does not block transfers. Relay uses its own minimum | A widget cannot stop oversized transfers or several small transfers accumulating; manual recovery is simpler than split-burn accounting; while the contracts are unaudited (D53), a default maximum caps what a bug could touch in one sweep, and hosts can raise it as they gain trust | Automatic multi-burn batches; a displayed cap without recovery; partial burns that leave an untracked remainder; sub-minimum sweeps | 2026-10-15 | |
| D29 | An own-rails checkout is paid when the USDC verified as arriving at its stored recipient on Base (D21), after the sweep fee and the CCTP fee, totals at least the price. The widget asks for the price plus the maximum fees ("Send 25.06 USDC"), a bound we can compute because the sweep fee is fixed (D39) and CCTP takes a maximum fee, so the recipient receives at least the price; anything above is recorded as surplus (as D25). A short total waits for a top-up to the same address; at 15 minutes it becomes `needs_attention: underpaid` (D17), and a later top-up still completes it | Own rails have no strict address, and exchanges often deduct their withdrawal fee from the amount; counting gross arrivals at the deposit address would mark orders paid while the merchant received less than the price | Exact amount only; judging each transfer on its own; counting gross arrivals at the deposit address; the merchant absorbing fees | 2026-10-15 | |
| D121 | Deposits are credited per full-balance sweep using chain execution order `(block number, transaction index, log position)`. A sweep owns incoming USDC transfers strictly after the preceding successful full-balance drain of that address and before its own sweep boundary, including transfers first discovered after workflow submission. A prior drain can be a sweep or an over-limit recovery, and need not already be credited. An incoming transfer is assigned to at most one drain and cannot be reassigned. Source transfers and the drain must be canonical at source `safe`; their gross sum must equal the emitted pre-drain balance, which must equal forwarded or burned amount plus the fixed sweep fee. Recovery has zero fee and is excluded from all credits. One sweep's uniquely matched net Base arrival makes one internal credit and one immutable `deposit.credited` event; actual HTTP delivery is at-least-once. Attribution failure becomes `needs_attention: attribution_mismatch` | A second deposit must not include the first sweep's transfers, and a recovered transfer must never fund a later credit | Every historical transfer before each sweep; grouping by workflow start time or recorded-at-submission snapshots; one credit per source transfer when several were swept together | 2026-10-15 | |
| D125 | Credits created at Base `safe` remain final business liabilities. The host accepts the residual loss risk if that evidence later becomes noncanonical. Reconciliation records the affected credit, original and current block evidence and missing backing in a critical incident; it pauses new credit creation on the affected chain until canonical reconciliation succeeds and an operator acknowledges the incident. It does not undo credits or issue replacement credits automatically. Verification-at-creation and continuing backing are distinct guarantees | Safe can still be affected by an Ethereum reorganization, while the business may already have honored the credit | Claiming safe is irreversible; silently editing credited amounts; automatically reversing host balances | 2026-10-15 | |
| D124 | The host atomically inserts a database-unique credit-event ID and updates its user balance in the same transaction. It acknowledges with 2xx only after commit, and returns 2xx without another balance update for an already committed ID. Handler failure before commit returns non-2xx. The reference integration implements this pattern and the receiver awaits the handler result; a signature check alone does not deduplicate a valid event. Event identity survives every retry and manual resend | A lost acknowledgment must not double-credit a user, and an early acknowledgment must not lose a credit | Optional deduplication; checking an ID separately from the balance transaction; returning success before commit | 2026-10-15 | |

## Architecture (D30–D39)

| ID | Decision | Why | Rejected | Date | Supersedes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D30 | Postgres with Drizzle in the host's own database, shipped with numbered SQL migrations that are never edited once published | Unique constraints, conditional updates and transactions are what make credit-once safe | Our own hosted database; a pluggable database layer | 2026-10-13 | |
| D31 | One schema covers orders and deposit addresses from the first migration | With one release, design the data once instead of reshaping it halfway | Checkout tables first, deposits added later | 2026-10-14 | |
| D32 | Payments move through a state machine with final states and compare-and-set transitions (update only if still in the expected state) | Two workers can't both move the same payment; final states never change | Free-form status updates | 2026-10-14 | |
| D33 | Own-rails contracts use Foundry and MIT code written from scratch. Each reusable account address (or slice-2 order address) is predicted with CREATE2 and the versioned shared factory (D134, D135). Its treasury recipient, refund address, gas-wallet fee recipient, source and destination configuration, minimum, sweep fee and Arbitrum automatic-processing maximum (D28) are committed by its creation code and initialized atomically. `sweep()` rejects zero and sub-minimum balances for every caller, then pays the stored fee and forwards the remaining USDC directly on Base or burns it through CCTP V2 Fast with Forwarding Service on Arbitrum. The Arbitrum contract rejects a whole-balance burn over D28's limits; it never splits or charges a fee on a rejected sweep. `recoverOverLimit()`, callable by anyone on Arbitrum only while either limit is exceeded, moves the entire USDC balance to the stored refund address with zero sweep fee. Every successful sweep emits its gross, fee and forwarded or burned amounts; every recovery emits its recovered amount and fixed destination. `rescue(token)`, callable by anyone, sends only non-USDC tokens or native ETH to the stored refund address. Repayment to the payer is the host's process (D27) | Every caller is bound to fixed destinations and minimums; emitted sweep and recovery evidence supports discovery without trusting the submitting worker | Caller-selected destinations or sweep amounts; a privileged sub-minimum path; automatic split burns; unrestricted USDC rescue; refund to a transaction sender | 2026-10-15 | |
| D34 | Our indexer reads incoming canonical USDC transfers and all successful sweep and over-limit recovery events for issued deposit contracts on Base and Arbitrum, regardless of caller or workflow. At each run it captures a `safe` block number and hash per chain, then reads every page of each range from that chain's committed position through the captured boundary. Events, deposits, drain memberships and that completed range's block-hash cursor are committed together; overlapping runs cannot move a cursor backwards. New address records store a scan start before any address is returned, and catch up from that start even if the global cursor has passed it. Targeted scans never advance the global cursor. Partial or failed ranges do not advance; records are deduplicated by chain, transaction and log index. Arbitrum deposit recording and automatic sweep eligibility stay at source `safe` in slice 1; S11 measures latency only, not permission to switch to `latest` | Find external sweeps and complete a finite captured range without claiming that safe can never reorganize | Incoming-transfer scans only; recording sources at latest; advancing on partial pages; using an address-only scan to skip global history | 2026-10-15 | |
| D35 | Discovery reconciliation restarts unfinished database operations as well as discovering payments. Books reconciliation matches every internal credit to its unique, canonical USDC arrival and verified net amount at its stored recipient on Base, and every treasury arrival to a credit or an open investigation. It also rechecks saved source and destination block hashes and cursors for canonicality. Noncanonical uncredited observations and memberships are invalidated and re-read from the last canonical cursor; credits remain final under D125 and their lost backing becomes a critical incident. A check is clean only when required scans and verification complete without open incidents (D54) | A valid credit at creation can later lose its backing, and an incomplete scan cannot prove clean books | Matching only transaction amounts or recipient balances; treating RPC failure or old results as clean; silently deleting credited evidence | 2026-10-15 | |
| D36 | Per-payment processes run as DBOS Transact workflows (MIT, state in the host Postgres): Relay settlement, one own-rails sweep operation at a time per address, and webhook delivery. The database records the completed stage, next action time, external transaction identities and worker claim. Scanning follows D131. Settle Kit pins the DBOS application version to its release, never the code hash, and preserves step ordering across compatible releases; incompatible changes use a new workflow name. Each process uses a unique executor ID and records a heartbeat at intervals no greater than 30 seconds; pending work from an executor with a heartbeat older than 120 seconds is recovered by the minute check. Each minute check resumes every unfinished operation with no runnable worker, regardless of current balance; a recorded burn resumes attestation or mint verification without another burn. Worker claims expire after 120 seconds and are renewed at most 30 seconds apart while executing; durable sleeps persist their next action and release the claim. A sleeping operation remains the address's open sweep. Work is recoverable when due and unclaimed, or when a claim expires; a `running` label alone cannot block recovery. Replacements use a new claim generation, and stale workers cannot commit stage advances; external effects remain idempotent under D139. The database, not workflow completion, is the source of truth | S12 establishes DBOS recovery and version/executor constraints; source balances become zero after burns, and crashed or stalled workers must not own work forever | Restarting at the burn stage from an empty address; assuming workflow labels or keys alone provide mutual exclusion | 2026-10-15 | |
| D37 | Settle Kit runs in any Node 20+ or Bun server process (Express, Fastify, Hono, Next.js, plain Node or Bun) or on serverless platforms, with any Postgres. No framework plugin or build step is required | Self-hosting is the point of the project, and the people who self-host use many stacks; S12 showed DBOS needs only a process and Postgres | Requiring a framework supported by the Workflow SDK (the earlier plan); Express and plain Node out of scope | 2026-10-15 | |
| D38 | Settle Kit chooses a lane per coin when issuing an address (USDC → own rails; USDT, ETH and wallet any-token payments → Relay), stores it and never changes it. There is no automatic fallback to Relay; a host may force future addresses per coin (D46). Before a successful CCTP burn, funds remain at the contract and the same sweep operation can retry. After a burn, recovery continues that message's attestation and mint verification (D138); it does not issue a new address or burn again. Over-limit funds use the fixed refund recovery in D28 | A permanent address defines the lane, but source funds no longer exist at the contract after a burn | Switching lanes after showing the address; pretending post-burn failures leave funds at the address | 2026-10-15 | |
| D39 | Each address stores a flat sweep fee in USDC base units, fixed at creation (default 0.01 USDC on Base, 0.05 USDC on Arbitrum). A configured fee must be nonnegative and strictly below that address's minimum; configuration and contract creation reject other values. A successful eligible sweep pays exactly that fee to its fixed gas-wallet address and sends or burns the remainder; rejected sweeps and over-limit recoveries charge zero USDC fee. An Arbitrum burn must leave a positive net amount after its maximum CCTP protocol and forwarding fee budget; an unaffordable route waits in attention without a burn. The gas wallet pays real gas in ETH and the host absorbs the difference. Wallet payments skip sweeps and use `permit` (EIP-2612) | Flat integer fees are exact to display and reconcile, while input validation prevents zero or negative principal and repeated sub-minimum fee collection | Measured gas refunds in USDC; caller-selected fee recipients; fees at or above the minimum; a fee on failed or over-limit recovery calls | 2026-10-15 | |
| D131 | On a long-running Node or Bun server, settle.start() registers DBOS schedules for indexing every 10 seconds and the rest of reconciliation every 60 seconds. On serverless, an authenticated POST cron handler runs indexing and reconciliation every 60 seconds. An open `<Deposit>` widget or mounted `useDeposits` hook checks its account's issued addresses every 5 seconds, including later deposits and top-ups after a previous transfer was recorded; these targeted scans share indexing and deduplication rules without advancing global cursors. A later RPC activity webhook may trigger the same scan but is not proof and is outside slice 1. S3's Base safe lag is a measured sample; Arbitrum safe lag remains to be measured in S11, so no fixed completion time is promised | A reusable address still needs observation after its first deposit, and source safe timing differs by chain | Polling only until the first transfer is recorded; treating one observed safe delay as a bound; an always-on scanning worker | 2026-10-15 | |
| D134 | One ownerless, non-upgradeable deposit factory per chain is deployed at the same address on Base and Arbitrum; a business may deploy its own from a supported version. The SDK ships a versioned chain manifest of chain IDs, token and CCTP contracts, factory runtime-code hashes and deposit creation-code hashes. Startup and address issuance verify the connected chain ID and factory runtime code against that manifest; missing or incompatible factories block issuance. An existing predicted contract is reused only if its version, immutable settings and code match the saved address record | Nonempty code is not proof that a factory can deploy the expected counterfactual address, and public predeployment must not be treated as an error | Existence-only checks; accepting unknown or upgradeable factories; trusting preexisting code at a predicted address | 2026-10-15 | |
| D135 | The CREATE2 salt uses an unambiguous encoding of a business namespace (32 random bytes generated once at setup) and account ID. The prediction also commits to the supported creation-code version and every immutable money setting in D33, including refund and fee recipients, source token, destination chain/domain and treasury. The complete prediction inputs and address are stored atomically under a unique business/account/route key before returning an address; concurrent first requests read back the same winner. Stored addresses are read back rather than re-derived from changed configuration | A shared factory must preserve account identity and bind initialization even if somebody deploys the address before our worker | Account-only salts; ambiguous string concatenation; initializing money settings after deployment; re-deriving existing addresses from current settings | 2026-10-15 | |
| D139 | Before broadcasting a deployment, sweep or manual recovery through the SDK, persist the operation, chain, signer, reserved nonce, signed transaction bytes and hash. Nonce reservation is database-unique per chain and signer across all addresses. A timeout, missing receipt or process crash is `outcome_unknown`, not a revert; reconcile the original transaction and on-chain events, or rebroadcast the same signed transaction. A fee replacement keeps the nonce, destination and call data, and records both hashes. A fresh-nonce attempt is allowed only after the previous attempt is proven reverted or its nonce proven consumed by another canonical transaction at source safe; preserve the same operation and recheck all execution preconditions | A retried workflow step must not turn an uncertain broadcast into another independently executable sweep | Saving the transaction hash only after the RPC responds; treating a timeout as proof of failure; per-address nonce allocation | 2026-10-15 | |
| D230 | Internal credit creation atomically claims the source memberships and unique Base arrival, inserts the final credit, and stores its immutable `deposit.credited` event with pending delivery in one Postgres transaction. Attention-state entry and its immutable event use the same transactional event path. The scheduled check starts or resumes pending delivery even for already credited deposits. Delivery identity and payload survive retries and manual resends; exhausted deliveries remain failed until operator resend under D45 | A process can die after a database credit commit and before starting a delivery workflow | Posting a webhook inside a database transaction; saving a credit and its event in separate commits; finding pending delivery only through uncredited deposits | 2026-10-15 | |
| D138 | Slice 1 relies on Circle Forwarding Service to handle attestation management, including expiry recovery, and submit the original burn's Base mint; Settle Kit does not run its own re-attestation loop. It retains the burn transaction, message identity and forwarding status, keeps tracking pending forwarding, and applies D17/D54 if it stalls or reports failure. A provider report or attestation alone never credits: identify the corresponding mint on Base and verify its token, recipient, net amount, message linkage and canonical safe receipt. Never create another burn to repair an existing one. Evidence is S13; Circle's published client assigns attestation recovery to the relayer, not a tested guarantee of recovery from every outage | Vinay accepts Circle's stated forwarding responsibility while settlement correctness remains independently verifiable | A second attestation recovery service in Settle Kit; crediting a forwarding status; burning again after a stalled mint | 2026-10-15 | |
