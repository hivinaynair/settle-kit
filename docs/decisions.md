| D17 | A payment enters `needs_attention` when a verified check finds an invalid outcome; a deposit also enters it for an over-limit balance, a failed forwarding operation or an unaffordable route fee. Funded attention cases leave only after verified settlement or the documented recovery resolution. An accepted wallet operation can also close as failed_no_debit under D111 on EVM or D233 on Solana. | D18 | The widget shows "Received" with a provisional amount when our own read finds a transfer at EVM `latest` or Solana `confirmed` (D232). | D110 | Both amount fields are editable and receive-first: changing You receive computes You pay; changing You pay computes You receive. Exactly one amount is authoritative per request. Show source chain, native USDC, fixed processing fee, total debit, exact quoted USDC credit and expiresAt before signing. A changed amount, wallet, account or network invalidates unsigned quote consent. Manual address mode shows estimates, never the locked-credit promise | The payer can choose their budget or target credit and understand the full price | Receive-only; pay-only; two inconsistent independently editable amounts | 2026-10-15 | |
| D111 | EVM wallet attempts distinguish prepared, accepted, outcome_unknown, source_succeeded and failed_no_debit; settlement and host delivery remain separate. expiresAt is the submission cutoff and validBefore is the signed on-chain execution deadline; both are persisted and shown or explained before consent. Acceptance cannot extend either. A failed attempt may retry the same authorization only within its original validity, spend budget and original operation identity. A revert alone is not terminal because the authorization may still execute. Nonce consumption alone is not proof of payment or cancellation. Close that payment attention episode without a credit and allow an explicitly confirmed new quote; keep unknown outcomes pending. No cancellation API or automatic re-signing is added | End a conclusively unsuccessful payment without permitting a second payment while the first can still execute | Infinite attention for expired unused payments; treating a revert or wall-clock expiry as proof of no debit | 2026-10-18 | |
| D112 | On entry or return, keep the initial authenticated history-loading view primary, then make unresolved accepted payments the primary view before a new-payment form. A connection/update failure during history loading is an error with Retry, never an empty history or evidence that no payment exists. Retain known attempts while retrying under continuing account authorization. Both entry methods remain available, but changing method does not recover, cancel or replace an unresolved attempt and must not present another transfer as its recovery | Returning users need continuity without an accidental second payment | A blank form while history is loading; interpreting a failed read as no deposits; switching methods as payment recovery | 2026-10-17 | |
| D113 | Before opening a live wallet signature request, explain that signing authorizes the displayed USDC debit and that the business pays network gas. Never silently refresh a signature or replace an accepted or uncertain attempt through this action | A signature is spending consent, and quote expiry should not erase the user's input or hide changed prices | Calling the signature a login or approval-free confirmation; clearing the form; automatic repricing, signing or payment replacement | 2026-10-17 | |
| D114 | Payer-facing exceptions explain the known outcome and next actor/action in plain words. Pending or uncertain execution says the original payment is being checked and warns against sending again. ## Money rules (D20–D29)

| ID | Decision | Why | Rejected | Date | Supersedes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D20 | The destination is native USDC on Base at the stored host treasury. Every issued address and prepared payment stores the destination chain, token and recipient and verification uses those saved values. Tokens are identified by chain/cluster and EVM contract or Solana mint plus token program, never symbol | Configuration changes cannot redirect an existing payment | Additional destination chains; client-selected treasury; symbol matching | 2026-10-18 | |
| D21 | Create internal credits only from independently verified canonical USDC backing at the stored Base recipient at or below a captured safe block. Manual sweeps credit their full verified net arrival (D121); accepted wallet quotes credit their fixed quoted amount using the exclusive allocation in D126/D127. Provider status, an authorization nonce, time elapsed or a host promise alone never authorizes credit. Retain receipt hashes and transfer identities for D125 | Exact quote accounting must remain backed by chain evidence | Credit at latest; fixed confirmation counts; unbacked shortfall credit | 2026-10-15 | |
| D22 | Amounts are base units: `bigint` in code, `numeric` in Postgres, decimal strings in JSON. Never floats | Floats lose cents | Floats; decimal libraries in the hot path | 2026-10-13 | |
| D23 | Database uniqueness covers source transfers, manual drains, payment IDs and EVM authorization nonces and Solana on-program payment receipts, destination transfers and backing allocations. Source identities are chain-qualified: EVM transaction/log index or Solana transaction signature plus outer/inner instruction position (D232). One chain transfer cannot fund two operations or credits. Claims, allocations, credit and its event commit atomically (D230). Host balance deduplication remains a separate atomic obligation (D124) | Manual scans, signed payment retries and webhook retries converge without double allocation | Application-only deduplication; treating delivery as exactly once | 2026-10-18 | |
| D26 | Treasury, fee and refund destinations come from host-server configuration and are stored with the address or quote. Authenticated account ownership is checked by the host; browser account IDs, recipient values or current config cannot override an issued destination | No browser request or later configuration change redirects funds | Client-selected recipients; verification against new settings | 2026-10-15 | |
| D27 | The SDK uses only host operational signing keys (EVM gas wallets, Solana fee payer and per-message CCTP event-account signers), never payer or treasury private keys. The host supplies ETH on EVM and SOL on Solana for relayed submissions, sweeps and account setup; collected USDC reimburses costs but is not automatically converted to ETH or SOL. The host absorbs gas from reverted submissions. Recovery pays only the stored host refund address; repayment to the payer remains the host support process. Quote shortfalls require verified host funding under D127, not control of customer wallets | Keep key custody and operating liabilities explicit | Payer seed import; automatic repayment to exchange sender addresses; assuming collected USDC funds ETH gas directly | 2026-10-18 | |
| D28 | Reusable manual deposit minimum is 2 USDC on Base, Arbitrum and Solana. Every caller, including the gas wallet, must meet the stored minimum to sweep; smaller deposits wait for top-up. Automatic Arbitrum and Solana processing also requires a gross address balance at most the address's stored maximum, fixed at creation (**default 1,000 USDC, 1,000,000,000 base units**; a host may configure its own) and a net burn no greater than Circle's current on-chain per-message burn limit. These are checked again at contract or program execution. Exceeding either limit blocks the entire automatic sweep and enters `needs_attention: over_limit`; no partial burn or sweep fee is charged. `recoverOverLimit()` may instead move the entire source-chain USDC balance to the stored host refund address without a sweep fee (D33, D234); recovery is manual and produces no Base credit. Base has no protocol processing maximum; a host-displayed guidance maximum does not block transfers. Wallet quotes check their own authorized gross amount against the stored route minimum and automatic-processing cap, plus the live net-burn limit, at preparation and contract/program execution; unrelated manual balances are not part of the quoted amount | A widget cannot stop oversized transfers or several small transfers accumulating; manual recovery is simpler than split-burn accounting; while the contracts are unaudited (D53), a default maximum bounds one automatic sweep, not aggregate funds at risk, and hosts can raise it as they gain trust | Automatic multi-burn batches; a displayed cap without recovery; partial burns that leave an untracked remainder; sub-minimum sweeps | 2026-10-18 | |
| D121 | Manual deposits are credited per full-balance sweep using EVM execution order `(block number, transaction index, log position)` or Solana finalized block transaction order and outer/inner instruction position (D232). A sweep owns manual incoming USDC transfers, excluding transfers consumed by a verified D231/D137 exact-amount operation, strictly after the preceding successful full-balance drain of that address and before its own sweep boundary, including transfers first discovered after workflow submission. A prior drain can be a sweep or an over-limit recovery, and need not already be credited. A manual incoming transfer is assigned to at most one drain and cannot be reassigned. Source transfers and the drain must be canonical at source `safe` on EVM or `finalized` on Solana; their gross sum must equal the emitted pre-drain balance, which must equal forwarded or burned amount plus the fixed sweep fee. Recovery has zero fee and is excluded from all credits. One sweep's uniquely matched net Base arrival makes one internal credit and one immutable `deposit.credited` event; actual HTTP delivery is at-least-once. Attribution failure becomes `needs_attention: attribution_mismatch` | A second manual deposit must not include the first sweep's transfers, and a recovered transfer must never fund a later credit | Every historical transfer before each sweep; grouping by workflow start time or recorded-at-submission snapshots; one credit per source transfer when several were swept together | 2026-10-18 | |
| D125 | Credits created at Base `safe` remain final business liabilities. The host accepts the residual loss risk if that evidence later becomes noncanonical. Reconciliation records the affected credit, original and current block evidence and missing backing in a critical incident; it pauses new credit creation on the affected chain until canonical reconciliation succeeds and an operator acknowledges the incident. It does not undo credits or issue replacement credits automatically. Verification-at-creation and continuing backing are distinct guarantees | Safe can still be affected by an Ethereum reorganization, while the business may already have honored the credit | Claiming safe is irreversible; silently editing credited amounts; automatically reversing host balances | 2026-10-15 | |
| D124 | The host atomically inserts a database-unique credit-event ID and updates its user balance in the same transaction. It acknowledges with 2xx only after commit, and returns 2xx without another balance update for an already committed ID. Handler failure before commit returns non-2xx. The reference integration implements this pattern and the receiver awaits the handler result; a signature check alone does not deduplicate a valid event. Event identity survives every retry and manual resend | A lost acknowledgment must not double-credit a user, and an early acknowledgment must not lose a credit | Optional deduplication; checking an ID separately from the balance transaction; returning success before commit | 2026-10-15 | |
| D126 | An accepted connected-wallet quote fixes total payer debit, processing fee and exact credit in USDC base units. The fee includes the route cost allowance; the host keeps unused allowance and absorbs overruns. It is a disclosed fixed processing fee, not a promise to refund actual gas differences. Verify gross debit, all source fees, burn/mint deductions and destination allocations; total debit equals quoted credit plus the disclosed fee. Record source-retained USDC, actual Circle deductions and net Base arrival separately; actual ETH/SOL transaction and Solana account-setup costs remain a host expense, not another customer debit. For Arbitrum and Solana, persist and bind the quoted credit, source-retained fee, burn amount and maximum combined Circle protocol/forwarding fee to consent. Require gross debit = source-retained fee + burn amount, and burn amount - maxFee >= quoted credit at preparation and contract/program execution. On Base require forwarded amount >= quoted credit. Refuse a quote or revert atomically if these bounds fail; do not plan routine host top-ups. One exclusive backing group is allocated to one payment: quoted credit plus separately recorded host residual equals verified Base backing, including any verified host shortfall funding. Manual sweeps still credit their full net arrival | Vinay chose fixed fees so the quoted credit stays exact | Returning savings as extra credits; reducing credit on an overrun; treating all treasury inflows as customer balance | 2026-10-18 | |
| D127 | A quote promises its amount conditional on a successful authorized payment, not a completion time. Once accepted for execution, fee variation cannot reduce its credit. Shortfall funding is an exceptional incident procedure, not a normal quote-pricing path or a general treasury subsystem. If verified net Base backing is short, enter needs_attention: quote_shortfall and retain the liability; do not create a partial or unbacked credit. The host funds the deficit from its own resources and supplies evidence through an authenticated operator procedure. Independently verify and exclusively link that Base transfer before crediting the fixed quote; excess host funding is recorded separately. No automatic withdrawal from the treasury, refund to payer, or second customer debit is implied. A reverted source transaction charges no USDC fee; a source-success/destination-stall keeps the original recovery identity | A fee guarantee must not weaken money backing or conceal a loss | Unbacked account credits; automatically re-signing or reburning; charging the payer for host overruns | 2026-10-15 | |
| D128 | Host configuration must provide finite transaction fee/gas ceilings, a maximum submission-attempt count per operation, per-account and payer submission limits, per-operation native-token spend budgets and deployment-wide per-chain native-token spend budgets over an explicit window, plus a minimum gas-wallet reserve. These values have no invented production defaults. Validate them before enabling live submission. Before each broadcast or fee replacement, atomically reserve its conservative maximum cost, including the EVM L2 data-fee allowance or Solana base/priority fees and account-creation rent, against shared persisted budgets; reconcile reservations against receipts without counting an EVM same-nonce replacement or Solana identical-byte rebroadcast as two simultaneous charges. Unknown attempts retain their reservation. Reject new acceptance when throttled or gas is unavailable; pause already accepted work with a visible reason without losing its identity. Continue read-only discovery, verification and delivery. Never raise the payer debit to fund a retry. Simulate before broadcast but do not treat simulation as protection against balance races. If cost bounds cannot be established, do not submit | Failed relays spend host ETH/SOL even when the payer pays no USDC; concurrent requests need one durable spending boundary | Unlimited free retries; per-process budgets; releasing unknown spend on timeout; simulation as an abuse control | 2026-10-18 | |
| D129 | An authenticated host operator may close a recovered-funds repayment case using either independently chain-verified repayment or explicitly host-reported repayment, including an off-chain payment. Store the case, operator identity, recording time, beneficiary, asset and amount, evidence reference and evidence kind. Chain verification establishes the transfer, not ownership of the beneficiary: the host confirms the intended beneficiary and that the evidence resolves the case. Host-reported evidence is never labeled chain-verified. Persist the resolution and alert closure atomically, retain the recovery and resolution history, and reject missing evidence or unauthorized closure. Moving funds to the host refund address is not repayment to the payer. This procedure cannot create a Base credit, alter a credit, or close an unrelated incident | Vinay chose both repayment routes with explicit evidence labels; support resolution must not masquerade as settlement proof | On-chain-only repayment; unlabeled operator assertions; closing an alert merely because funds reached the host | 2026-10-15 | |

## Architecture (D30–D39)

| ID | Decision | Why | Rejected | Date | Supersedes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D30 | Postgres with Drizzle in the host's own database, shipped with numbered SQL migrations that are never edited once published | Unique constraints, conditional updates and transactions are what make credit-once safe | Our own hosted database; a pluggable database layer | 2026-10-13 | |
| D31 | The schema covers deposit addresses, manual transfers/drains, wallet quotes and payment attempts, verified backing allocations, credits, outbox delivery and recovery evidence. No order tables or unused provider abstractions are added | Persist each concrete recovery and accounting fact needed by this release | Designing checkout and Relay tables before they are used | 2026-10-15 | |
| D32 | Payments move through a state machine with final states and compare-and-set transitions (update only if still in the expected state) | Two workers can't both move the same payment; final states never change | Free-form status updates | 2026-10-14 | |
| D33 | EVM own-rails contracts use Foundry and MIT code written from scratch. Each reusable account address is predicted with CREATE2 and the versioned shared factory (D134, D135). Its treasury recipient, refund address, gas-wallet fee recipient, source and destination configuration, minimum, sweep fee and Arbitrum automatic-processing maximum (D28) are committed by its creation code and initialized atomically. `sweep()` rejects zero and sub-minimum balances for every caller, then pays the stored fee and forwards the remaining USDC directly on Base or burns it through CCTP V2 Fast with Forwarding Service on Arbitrum. The Arbitrum contract rejects a whole-balance burn over D28's limits; it never splits or charges a fee on a rejected sweep. `recoverOverLimit()`, callable by anyone on Arbitrum only while either limit is exceeded, moves the entire USDC balance to the stored refund address with zero sweep fee. D231 adds an exact-amount authorization entry point; it is not a full-balance sweep and does not consume unrelated manual funds. Every successful sweep emits its gross, fee and forwarded or burned amounts; every recovery emits its recovered amount and fixed destination. `rescue(token)`, callable by anyone, sends only non-USDC tokens or native ETH to the stored refund address. Every successful rescue emits its token (or native-ETH marker), amount and fixed refund recipient; a failed transfer reverts without a success event. Repayment to the payer is the host's process (D27, D129) | Every caller is bound to fixed destinations and minimums; emitted sweep and recovery evidence supports discovery without trusting the submitting worker | Caller-selected destinations or sweep amounts; a privileged sub-minimum path; automatic split burns; unrestricted USDC rescue; refund to a transaction sender | 2026-10-18 | |
| D34 | For Base and Arbitrum, our indexer reads incoming canonical USDC transfers and all successful sweep, quoted-payment, non-USDC/native-ETH rescue and over-limit recovery events for issued deposit contracts on Base and Arbitrum, regardless of caller or workflow. At each run it captures a `safe` block number and hash per chain, then reads every page of each range from that chain's committed position through the captured boundary. Events, deposits, drain memberships and that completed range's block-hash cursor are committed together; overlapping runs cannot move a cursor backwards. New address records store a scan start before any address is returned, and catch up from that start even if the global cursor has passed it. Targeted scans never advance the global cursor. Partial or failed ranges do not advance; records are deduplicated by chain, transaction and log index. Manual Arbitrum recording and automatic sweep eligibility stay at source `safe`; quoted source execution must also be canonical at source `safe` before settlement credit; S11 measures latency only, not permission to switch to `latest` | Find external sweeps and complete a finite captured range without claiming that safe can never reorganize | Incoming-transfer scans only; recording sources at latest; advancing on partial pages; using an address-only scan to skip global history | 2026-10-18 | |
| D35 | Discovery reconciliation restarts unfinished database operations as well as discovering payments.
