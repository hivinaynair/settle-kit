# Daimo's deposit playbook sharpens Settle Kit

Daimo (called Daimo Pay until early 2026) works like a courier who pays the recipient out of pocket and collects from the post office later. Every payment gets a contract address that can be computed before the contract exists. A Daimo-run bot called a relayer sweeps whatever lands at that address, swaps and bridges it to the destination chain, pays the recipient from its own funds within seconds, and repays itself when the slower bridge transfer arrives. Around February 2026 Daimo rebuilt the product as a hosted, sales-gated deposit ramp (a service that turns whatever a user holds into the app's stablecoin): the integrator's server creates a session, the payer moves through six statuses, three signed webhook events report progress, and a country-aware picker now leads with Apple Pay, bank transfers and exchanges rather than crypto wallets. The React SDK and Solidity contracts are public, but the relayer, price signer, API, webhooks and all fiat, Tron and Solana handling are closed, and the contract generation live today accepts completion only from Daimo-approved relayers and has no public audit. For Settle Kit the headline is reassuring: the deposit-kit design already beats Daimo where integrators get hurt most, durable tracking and duplicate-credit prevention, because Daimo dropped idempotent payment creation in its 2026 rebuild. Settle Kit lags on conventions Daimo has hardened in production: a closed set of final statuses, an event catalogue that separates estimated from received amounts, refunds that never target an exchange, and a concrete retry schedule. The most valuable changes are to write those conventions into §5, §7, §9 and §10 of the [design doc](../docs/plans/2026-10-13-deposit-kit-design.md), resolve two Relay unknowns (deposits where source and destination are the same chain and token, and what happens to stuck funds if Relay stops), and give the §12a own-address roadmap Daimo's contract safety rules without its trust regressions.

## Daimo pays the recipient first and collects from the bridge later

### One address per payment, computed before the contract exists

On EVM chains (Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum and the other chains that run Ethereum's contract code), a rule called CREATE2 lets anyone compute a contract's address before deploying it, by hashing the deployer, a salt and the contract's code with its startup settings. Daimo puts the payment's parameters into that startup data. The address works like a pre-printed envelope whose instructions cannot be changed: a payer can send ordinary tokens to it immediately, and the contract deployed there later can only move the money the way those parameters say ([DepositAddressFactory.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DepositAddressFactory.sol)). Each address is a proxy (a tiny contract that borrows its logic from a shared implementation) storing **one hash of its parameters**, and only Daimo's manager contract can make it release funds ([DepositAddress.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DepositAddress.sol)).

The repo holds three contract generations, and public descriptions of Daimo often mix them up. The first, Pay Intent, baked the amount and a nonce (a one-time counter) into the address, so each address served exactly one payment, and anyone could complete it ([PayIntent.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/PayIntent.sol)). The second, Universal Address, was audited in July 2025 and **deleted from the repo on January 8, 2026** ([commit 99c3f24](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/commit/99c3f24)). The third, Deposit Address, added in December 2025 and the target of every recent contract commit, drops both amount and nonce so one address can be reused, and adds Solana and Tron destinations ([commit c990a19](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/commit/c990a19); [DepositAddress.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DepositAddress.sol)).

| Generation | Active | Address | Who can move funds forward | Public audit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pay Intent | 2024 to 2025 | One-time; commits amount and nonce | Anyone | Nethermind NM-0500, April 2025 |
| Universal Address | Mid 2025, deleted January 2026 | Backed by an upgradeable shared config with a global pause | Anyone could refund or complete | Nethermind NM-0585, July 2025 |
| Deposit Address (live) | December 2025 to now | Reusable; no amount or nonce; has an expiry | Daimo-allowlisted relayers only; anyone can refund after expiry | None found |

### Start, fast finish, claim

A plain token transfer to a Daimo address does nothing by itself. An off-chain relayer watches the address and calls the manager contract, which runs the payment in three moves ([DepositAddressManager.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DepositAddressManager.sol)). In `start`, on the source chain, the relayer pulls the **whole balance** out of the deposit address, swaps it into a bridge token, and sends it across a bridge (a protocol that moves tokens between chains) to a fresh per-transfer receiver on the destination chain. That transfer's ID, a hash of the deposit address, a relayer-chosen salt, the bridged amount and the source chain, doubles as a replay guard so one transfer cannot be paid twice. In `fastFinish`, on the destination chain, the relayer pays the recipient from its own inventory right away and is recorded as owed. In `claim`, once the slow bridge lands (the code comments cite 10+ minutes for CCTP, Circle's USDC bridge), the relayer is repaid from the bridged funds, or, if no fast finish happened, the funds go straight to the recipient ([DaimoPay.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DaimoPay.sol)).

### Bridges are bought, not built

Routing is a whitelist fixed when the bridger contract is deployed: each allowed combination of destination type, chain, token and adapter is set in the constructor and cannot change ([DepositAddressBridger.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DepositAddressBridger.sol)). Current route families are CCTP v2 for USDC; LayerZero-based token bridges (Stargate, USDT0 and the USDT0 Legacy Mesh) for USDT and others; a two-leg hop through **USDC on Arbitrum** for chains without a direct route; the 0x cross-chain aggregator for some BNB Chain, Optimism and Monad routes; and **Relay for USDC to Solana and USDT to Tron** ([DeployDepositAddressBridger.s.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/script/da/DeployDepositAddressBridger.s.sol); [DAHopBridgeRouteConstants.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/script/da/constants/DAHopBridgeRouteConstants.sol)). Across and Axelar, which powered the first generation, have no routes in the live one. Relay delivery for Solana and Tron landed in early October 2026 ([daimo-eth/sdk commits](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/commits/master)), so Daimo now buys some of its routing from the same router Settle Kit plans to use.

### Signed prices replace committed amounts

Because live addresses no longer commit to an amount, the contracts bound the output another way. A Daimo key signs USD prices, the `DaimoPayPricer` contract rejects stale or unsigned ones, and each address carries maximum slippage caps in basis points (one basis point is 0.01%) for the start, fast-finish and same-chain steps ([DaimoPayPricer.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DaimoPayPricer.sol); [DepositAddress.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DepositAddress.sol)). Within those caps the relayer **keeps any swap surplus at the start step**, while the fast-finish step hands the recipient the entire swap output ([DepositAddressManager.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DepositAddressManager.sol)). The 0x adapter's own comments say the recipient absorbs the gap between quoted and minimum output ([DAZeroXBridger.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DAZeroXBridger.sol)). Daimo's private relayer contract can top up small shortfalls and refund overpayments, but those are per-call operating choices, not protocol guarantees ([DaimoPayRelayer.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/relayer/DaimoPayRelayer.sol)).

### Completion moved from anyone to Daimo's relayers

The first generation let anyone finish a payment if no relayer showed up, which is how Daimo justified saying neither it nor liquidity providers ever controlled user funds ([legacy How it works](https://web.archive.org/web/20250129193627/https://paydocs.daimo.com/how-it-works)). In the live generation, every function that moves funds forward (`start`, `fastFinish`, `claim`, `sameChainFinish`, `hopStart`, `refundFulfillment`) requires a caller on an allowlist that only the contract owner can edit, and the deploy script makes the deployer the owner ([DepositAddressManager.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DepositAddressManager.sol); [DeployDepositAddressManager.s.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/script/da/DeployDepositAddressManager.s.sol)). The owner cannot redirect funds, because parameters are hash-committed and deposit addresses have no upgrade function. But if Daimo's relayers stop, money sits until the address expires, and **the payer's only unilateral exit is `refundDepositAddress`, which anyone may call after expiry**. Daimo has published no reason for the change, and whether the owner is a single key or a multisig is unknown. The non-custodial claim still holds in the narrow sense that nobody can steal, but liveness now depends entirely on Daimo.

### Finality is the relayer's risk, and Daimo does not publish its policy

A chain occasionally rewrites its most recent blocks (a reorg), which can erase a payment that looked confirmed; finality is the point after which that can no longer happen. **Nothing in Daimo's contracts waits for confirmations** ([DepositAddressManager.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DepositAddressManager.sol)). The relayer pays before the source chain is final, so if the source transaction is reorged away after a fast finish, the relayer is never repaid and the recipient keeps the money. Old docs framed this as the liquidity provider taking reorg and bridge risk in exchange for a fee ([legacy How it works](https://web.archive.org/web/20250129193627/https://paydocs.daimo.com/how-it-works)). Confirmation counts per chain are unpublished, and the legacy webhook docs said Daimo did not notify on reorgs ([legacy Webhooks, March 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250322222241/https://paydocs.daimo.com/webhooks)). The same split applies to Settle Kit: Relay's solvers carry source-chain risk, so Settle Kit only needs destination finality, which its `safe`-block check already covers.

## Refunds go to one committed address, which is where money gets lost

Every refund in every generation goes to the single `refundAddress` committed in the address parameters. In the first generation, `refundIntent` was open to anyone once a payment had started, been claimed or expired; it covered double payments and anything arriving after a claim, and a reverted final contract call also sent funds to the refund address ([DaimoPay.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DaimoPay.sol)). In the live generation, `refundDepositAddress` lets relayers refund at any time and anyone refund after expiry, and a relayer-only `refundFulfillment` recovers bridged funds stuck at a destination receiver ([DepositAddressManager.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DepositAddressManager.sol)). An audit finding shows why this needs care: in the deleted Universal Address design, stablecoin payments with an invalid route could fail and **could not be refunded** until Nethermind flagged it (finding 6.2, fixed) ([NM-0585 report](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/audit/NM-0585-Daimo-final-report.pdf)).

Wrong-token, wrong-chain and amount mistakes are handled mostly off-chain and mostly without documentation. Reading the code, any token with a Daimo-signed price gets swapped, while an unpriced token can leave only through the refund function. A deposit sent on a different EVM chain lands at the same computed address and is refundable only if Daimo's factory exists there; the repo ships a CREATE3 deployer (a variant that gives a contract the same address on every chain regardless of its code) that makes this likely for chains Daimo has deployed to ([DepositAddressManager.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DepositAddressManager.sol); [script directory](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/tree/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/script)). There is no on-chain minimum: the relayer bridges whatever arrived, and the hosted API decides whether a session counts as paid. Current docs say only that a slight shortfall or excess from bridge fees or price moves still completes, with the variance visible in the session and webhooks ([Advanced Sessions](https://docs.daimo.com/advanced/sessions)). Overpayment once favored the relayer: the first generation's bug bounty treated a solver keeping an extra $50 as known behavior ([Immunefi](https://immunefi.com/bug-bounty/daimo-pay/)).

Daimo's refund-address policy evolved because exchanges break the simple rule of refunding to the sender. In early 2025 it promised automatic refunds to the customer but told merchants to handle exchange payers manually ([legacy Payment Links](https://web.archive.org/web/20250318201907/https://paydocs.daimo.com/payment-links)). By January 2026 it refunded to the connected wallet in the wallet flow and to a developer-supplied fallback in the manual address flow, explaining: "We cannot reliably refund to exchange/custodial deposit addresses" ([legacy SDK reference, January 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260117070417/https://paydocs.daimo.com/sdk)). An exchange deposit address belongs to the exchange, so a refund sent there may never reach the user. The current API accepts only an EVM refund address and describes it in terms of reverted contract calls; behavior for underpaid, expired or wrong-token sessions is undocumented, and **no on-chain check stops a refund to an address that cannot receive the token** ([OpenAPI](https://docs.daimo.com/openapi.json)).

The payer-facing story moved from explicit to silent. The deprecated SDK showed a Tron-specific screen telling the payer their USDT payment was too low, that their funds were safe, and to email support for a refund ([@daimo/pay on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@daimo/pay)). The current SDK has no underpay wording at all; it tolerates small variance and leaves large mistakes to refunds after the one-hour address expires ([en.ts](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/web/hooks/locales/en.ts); [WaitingDepositAddressPage.tsx](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/web/components/WaitingDepositAddressPage.tsx)). Neither extreme is good: the first strands the payer in an email thread, the second leaves them with no explanation.

## Six statuses, three webhooks and no idempotency key

### Sessions pick checkout or deposit by whether an amount is present

The integrator's server calls `POST /v1/sessions` with a secret API key, naming a destination (an EVM chain and token, Solana USDC or Tron USDT, the last two enabled per organization), display text (a title and a one-word verb), optional string metadata and an optional refund address ([OpenAPI](https://docs.daimo.com/openapi.json); [Custom Integration](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/custom-integration)). The response carries a session ID and a client secret, a per-session token that is safe in the browser because it unlocks only that session ([API Overview](https://docs.daimo.com/api-reference/overview)). When the payer picks a method, the client registers it and receives a **single-use EVM receiver address**, a temporary Tron address, or a ready-made Solana transaction to sign ([Sessions](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/sessions)). Mode follows one rule inherited from the old SDK: set `amountUnits` and it is a fixed-amount checkout; omit it and the payer chooses (an open-amount deposit); add `calldata` (encoded instructions for a contract call) and Daimo approves the tokens to a contract and calls it ([Advanced Sessions](https://docs.daimo.com/advanced/sessions); [legacy SDK reference, January 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260117070417/https://paydocs.daimo.com/sdk)). Even the new withdrawal widget is an open-amount session underneath ([Withdrawals](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/withdrawals)).

### Final states never change, and the estimate is not the amount

Current sessions run `requires_payment_method`, then `waiting_payment`, then `processing`, then `succeeded` or `bounced`, with `expired` reachable from the first two. **The three end states are final and a session never leaves them**, and the SDK exports helpers such as `isSessionTerminal` and `isSessionStarted` ([Sessions](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/sessions); [session.d.ts](https://unpkg.com/@daimo/sdk@1.8.8/dist/common/session.d.ts)). `bounced` means delivery failed, typically a reverted contract call, and the funds went to the refund address; the session's delivery record then describes the refund transaction rather than the payment. The legacy product used `payment_unpaid`, `payment_started`, `payment_completed`, `payment_bounced` and later `payment_refunded`, while keeping finer source and destination sub-states (fast finish submitted, claimed and so on) internal ([pay-common daimoPay.ts](https://unpkg.com/@daimo/pay-common@1.19.11/src/daimoPay.ts)).

| What it means | Daimo today | Daimo legacy | Settle Kit design (§5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waiting for the payer | `requires_payment_method`, `waiting_payment` | `payment_unpaid` | `waiting` |
| Deposit seen, being routed | `processing` | `payment_started` | `processing`, `delayed` |
| Funds verified at destination | folded into `succeeded` | `payment_completed` | `settled` |
| App applied the credit | no equivalent | no equivalent | `credited` |
| Delivery failed, money returned | `bounced` | `payment_bounced`, `payment_refunded` | `refunded` |
| Stopped without delivery | `expired` | expiry timestamp only | `failed`, `needs_attention` |

Daimo also separates what it expects from what arrived. A processing snapshot carries `expectedUnits`, an estimate that moves with price, slippage, fees or subsidies, while `delivery.receivedUnits` is the authoritative figure after delivery ([Webhooks](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/webhooks)). The legacy SDK promised exactly `toUnits` at the destination ([legacy SDK reference, March 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250322215521/https://paydocs.daimo.com/sdk)). The current docs still describe `amountUnits` as fixed but tell integrators to expect slight variance and to have contracts pull the full approved amount, so reconciliation should key on the received figure ([Advanced Sessions](https://docs.daimo.com/advanced/sessions)).

### Webhooks are signed, retried for about 17 hours, and deduplicated by event ID

An HMAC signature is a fingerprint computed from a message and a shared secret, so the receiver can check who sent it and that nobody altered it. Current webhooks cover `session.processing`, `session.succeeded` and `session.bounced`, each delivering an event ID, type, timestamp and **a full session snapshot**, plus an `isTestEvent` flag on test sends ([Webhooks](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/webhooks)). Daimo signs `${t}.${rawBody}` with HMAC-SHA256 in a `Daimo-Signature: t=…,v1=…` header and tells receivers to compare in constant time and **reject timestamps older than five minutes**. It waits 10 seconds for any 2xx response, retries 10 times with the nth retry waiting 2^(n-1) minutes (about 17 hours in total, by arithmetic), then marks the event failed; receivers dedupe on the event ID. The secret is shown in full only at creation, and a test endpoint sends sample events ([OpenAPI](https://docs.daimo.com/openapi.json)). There is no event for expiry or for a payer choosing a method, so expiry requires polling, and the current docs are silent on ordering and reorgs. The legacy system was weaker: a static `Authorization: Basic` token, retries for up to a day, an `Idempotency-Key` header, and an explicit warning that a completed event could arrive before a started one ([legacy Webhooks, March 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250322222241/https://paydocs.daimo.com/webhooks); [legacy Webhooks, February 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260210214445/https://paydocs.daimo.com/webhooks)). A late legacy `payment_refunded` event carried the refund address, chain, token, transaction and amount, and warned that chain and token need not match the original payment.

### Idempotency went backwards, and attribution now rides on metadata

Idempotency means a repeated request has the same effect as one request, so a retry after a timeout cannot create a duplicate. The legacy Payments API had it: creating again with the same `externalId` returned the original payment, and a lookup endpoint found payments by that ID ([legacy Payments API, April 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250418220344/https://paydocs.daimo.com/payments-api)). Those features vanished from the docs in January 2026 ([legacy Payments API, January 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260117062224/https://paydocs.daimo.com/payments-api)), and **the current OpenAPI spec contains no idempotency key or external ID**, so every create call mints a new session ([OpenAPI](https://docs.daimo.com/openapi.json)). Integrators must save the session ID against their own order before handing it to the browser, or put their order ID in `metadata`, a string map echoed in webhooks (legacy limits were 50 pairs of up to 500 characters; current limits are undocumented) ([pay-common daimoPay.ts](https://unpkg.com/@daimo/pay-common@1.19.11/src/daimoPay.ts)). Attribution itself comes from the single-use receiver address: one address, one session, and once a deposit is detected the session records the payer's source address, chain, token, amount and transaction ([API Overview](https://docs.daimo.com/api-reference/overview)). The legacy types also show a source-screening check with `ok` and `blocked` results, the same job as Settle Kit's `address_blocked` error.

### The SDK is a thin shell, and the server holds the truth

`@daimo/sdk`, first published February 28, 2026, offers `DaimoFrame`, a hosted iframe that **needs no wallet libraries**, a lower-level `DaimoModal`, a React Native WebView wrapper, and a raw WebView protocol using a versioned `postMessage` envelope ([npm @daimo/sdk](https://registry.npmjs.org/@daimo/sdk); [Web guide](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/modal); [Native WebView](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/webview-native)). Its callbacks `onPaymentStarted` and `onPaymentCompleted` take no arguments and there is no bounce callback; the docs tell integrators to poll the session or use webhooks for anything real ([hooks/types.d.ts](https://unpkg.com/@daimo/sdk@1.8.8/dist/web/hooks/types.d.ts)). The modal polls, and a `check` call accepts an optional transaction hash to speed up detection ([Sessions](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/sessions)). The deprecated `@daimo/pay` took the opposite approach: a fork of ConnectKit (a wallet-connection UI kit) with wagmi and viem as required peers, and callback payloads identical to webhooks ([npm @daimo/pay](https://registry.npmjs.org/@daimo/pay); [legacy SDK reference, March 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20250322215521/https://paydocs.daimo.com/sdk)). Theming moved to dashboard-managed color and radius tokens ([Customization](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/customization)).

### Fees are negotiated and mostly invisible

Daimo publishes no price list. The only concrete figure is from a February 2025 Devcon proposal: **a flat 1% paid by the merchant**, covering relayers, swaps, bridges and gas, with payments from Ethereum mainnet costing the payer more ([DIP-64](https://forum.devcon.org/t/dip-64-universal-checkout-for-devcon-nect/5346)). The current SDK can show a flat fee and the resulting amount received for some exchange and fiat methods ([SelectAmountPage.tsx](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/web/components/SelectAmountPage.tsx)), and Circle's partner page claims users never see fees, which is marketing ([Circle partner page](https://partners.circle.com/partner/daimo-pay)). Settle Kit's model of no fee beyond Relay's is simpler to explain, but it exposes Relay's fees directly, which is why the design sets a $2 minimum.

## A server-driven picker funnels every payer into one progress screen

The modal renders a navigation tree sent by the server, not a hardcoded menu. Node types cover option lists, deposit addresses, deeplinks (links that open a specific screen in another app), exchanges, Cash App, Stripe, Tron, connected wallets and fiat, and **any option can be shown disabled with a reason** ([navTree.ts](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/web/api/navTree.ts)). In automatic mode Daimo orders methods by the payer's country: US payers see Apple Pay, bank transfer, Cash App and Coinbase first, and unrecognized countries get Binance USDT, Coinbase and USDT on Tron. A fixed mode pins one of 18 methods and skips the picker entirely ([Payment Methods](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/payment-methods)). A rail with an outage stays visible but leads to a temporarily-unavailable screen, the payer can change country, and the UI ships in six languages ([en.ts](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/web/hooks/locales/en.ts); [locale.ts](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/web/hooks/locale.ts)). The point is that Daimo can change methods, order, limits and wording per country without anyone shipping a new SDK.

The deposit-address screen names the chain, labels the address as one-time, shows the amount, toggles a QR code, counts down to expiry, disables copying once expired, and offers to generate a new address; **addresses live 3,600 seconds** ([WaitingDepositAddressPage.tsx](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/web/components/WaitingDepositAddressPage.tsx)). The default token hint is USDT or USDC, and Tron responses can include a Trust Wallet deeplink prefilled for the send ([common/api.ts](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/common/api.ts)). The short lifetime stops payers from reusing stale addresses, and the post-expiry refund backs it up.

Exchanges are handed off, never connected by API. Coinbase, Mt Pelerin and Revolut open a provider popup on desktop; Binance, Bitget, Bybit, Lemon and Cash App show a QR code to scan with a phone; on mobile both kinds deeplink straight into the app ([ExchangePage.tsx](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/web/components/ExchangePage.tsx)). The amount is chosen first, and the embedded Coinbase widget's own errors are explicitly never treated as evidence of payment ([common/api.ts](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/common/api.ts)). Some exchange flows return Lightning invoices (Bitcoin's fast payment layer) with an expiry and a refresh action.

Fiat runs through a Daimo Account: an emailed one-time code, terms, then identity checks (KYC, know-your-customer verification) scaled to the rail, from none for JPYC, to a phone check for Apple and Google Pay, an ID for ACH, SEPA and Argentine pesos, and ID plus selfie for Interac and Colombia's Bre-B ([Fiat](https://docs.daimo.com/guides/fiat)). Screens **name the third-party partner the payer will see in their bank app**, such as PayTrie for Interac, and Apple Pay runs inside a Coinbase frame with a $500 default cap when the server sends none ([en.ts](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/web/hooks/locales/en.ts); [AccountWalletPayPage.tsx](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/web/components/account/AccountWalletPayPage.tsx)).

Every path ends in the same progress screen: confirm, waiting for payment, payment received, processing and completed, plus refunding, bounced and expired states, with actions to show a receipt, return to the app or contact support ([ConfirmationPage.tsx](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/master/packages/sdk/src/web/components/ConfirmationPage.tsx)). Since July 2026, reopening a paid session resumes its status instead of starting over ([daimo-eth/sdk commits](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/commits/master)). Settle Kit's §9 screens (detected, processing, credited; then credited, refunded, needs attention or failed; recent deposits on reopen) already cover the same ground, and Daimo adds one useful extra: a distinct in-progress refunding state, so a payer whose money is on its way back is not told it is already back.

## Open contracts, a closed engine, and a pivot to fiat ramps

Everything public sits in one repo, `daimo-eth/sdk`. The TypeScript SDK is BSD-2-Clause (a permissive license that allows reuse with attribution), but **every Solidity file carries a GPL-3.0-or-later header** (a copyleft license that requires derivative code to stay GPL), while the repo-level `license.txt` says BSD-2-Clause and the root `LICENSE` is a broken symlink, so GitHub cannot classify the repo ([license.txt](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/license.txt); [DaimoPay.sol](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/src/DaimoPay.sol); [github.com/daimo-eth](https://github.com/daimo-eth)). Anyone reusing the Solidity should assume GPL. Closed and hosted: the relayer service, indexer, price and quote signers, the `/v1` API, webhooks, and all fiat, exchange, Tron and Solana source handling. The older `daimo-eth/pay` repo now returns 404.

Nethermind audited the first generation (report dated April 25, 2025; findings not retrievable) and the Universal Address generation (final July 31, 2025: **no critical, high or medium findings**, two low and one best-practice, all fixed) ([NM-0585 report](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/contract/audit/NM-0585-Daimo-final-report.pdf)). Daimo then deleted the audited code and replaced it with Deposit Address contracts that add the relayer allowlist, signed prices, Solana and Tron delivery, the hop bridge, Relay and 0x adapters, and permit-based pulls (EIP-2612 permits let a wallet authorize a transfer with a signature instead of a separate transaction). **No public audit of the live code was found**, so treat it as unaudited ([commit 99c3f24](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/commit/99c3f24)). Veridise's 2023 audits covered the old wallet, not payments ([Veridise](https://veridise.com/audits-archive/company/daimo-pay/daimo-2023-10-06/)). The bug bounty's status is unclear: one researcher found an Immunefi listing offering up to $20,000, live since June 2025 and updated March 2026 ([Immunefi information](https://immunefi.com/bug-bounty/daimo-pay/information/)), while another found the program and scope pages returning 404 on October 14, 2026 ([Immunefi](https://immunefi.com/bug-bounty/daimo-pay/)). No exploit or postmortem turned up.

Docs list 12 destination chains (Arbitrum, Base, BNB Chain, Celo, Ethereum, HyperEVM, Linea, Monad, Optimism, Polygon, Worldchain, and Solana for USDC only), with USDT, DAI and ETH also available on most EVM chains ([Supported Chains](https://docs.daimo.com/supported-chains)). The API also accepts Tron USDT destinations, enabled per organization ([OpenAPI](https://docs.daimo.com/openapi.json)). Sources are any liquid ERC-20 token (the standard EVM token format) on those chains, Solana USDC, USDT, SOL and other liquid tokens, and Tron USDT. The SDK's own chain list adds Arc, MegaETH and Tempo and has retired Gnosis and Scroll ([chain.ts](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/blob/030edf8a2f9457f32fadd6f8a01eef21e07e18ae/packages/sdk/src/common/chain.ts)).

The pivot shows in packages and pages. `@daimo/pay` was deprecated on February 24, 2026, `@daimo/sdk` shipped four days later, and the old docs now redirect to the new ones ([npm @daimo/pay](https://registry.npmjs.org/@daimo/pay)). The homepage pitches Daimo as a ramp for neobanks, with per-user local bank accounts and rails including Interac, Apple and Google Pay, ACH, SEPA, JPYC, Argentine pesos, Bre-B and PIX (coming), and shows Opera, Tria, EtherFi and World logos ([daimo.com](https://daimo.com)). 2026 commits went mostly to fiat, KYC, a withdrawal widget, React Native and Relay delivery ([daimo-eth/sdk commits](https://github.com/daimo-eth/sdk/commits/master)), while the ticketing plugin and store demo were archived ([github.com/daimo-eth](https://github.com/daimo-eth)). Signs of scale are modest: about **7,700 monthly npm downloads** for `@daimo/sdk` ([npm downloads](https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-month/@daimo/sdk)), a team of about five according to one aggregator ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/daimo/__0oWfevITQC2RVfxkCLk6rcWZBWMPXJ5W38NiT-J9o2c)), and EtherFi, one of the displayed logos, announced MoonPay's full stack for its neobank ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/etherfi-deploys-moonpays-full-infrastructure-stack-to-bring-money-in-around-and-out-of-a-self-custodial-neobank-302900288.html)). Read together, crypto from any chain is now table stakes inside a fiat-first product, and Daimo's claimed edge is localization and partner rails, not routing.

## What Settle Kit should copy, adapt and avoid

Settle Kit's design borrows Relay's reusable deposit addresses (one per account and route), detects fills through signed Relay webhooks backed by periodic reconciliation, verifies arrival on the destination chain at the `safe` block, and asks the host to credit with a stable deposit ID. Against Daimo it is **already stronger in four places**: address issuance is idempotent through a unique key on account, router, network and route; a send-attempt key prevents double sends; two unique keys stop a fill or an on-chain transfer from being recorded twice; and credit waits for on-chain verification. Daimo offers none of the first three to integrators today. The gaps are in conventions and in a few unresolved Relay behaviors.

| Topic | Daimo | Settle Kit design today | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Final statuses | Three end states that never change; helper functions | Statuses listed, no transition table or final set | Copy |
| Event payload | Event ID, full snapshot, estimate vs received amount | `deposit.credited` with the deposit; other events implied | Copy |
| Delivery retries | 10 retries, doubling from 1 minute | Open question in §13 | Copy |
| Signature freshness | Reject older than 5 minutes | Freshness check, no number | Copy |
| Fast detection | `check` with a transaction hash hint | Scan on widget reopen | Copy as an endpoint |
| Address lifetime | Single-use, 1 hour, then refundable by anyone | Reusable, reconciled forever | Adapt |
| Refund target | Wallet for wallet flow; developer fallback for manual flow | `refundTo` and `recoveryAddress`; rule unclear for a shared address | Adapt |
| Method list | Server-driven, per country, disabled with reasons | Two fixed methods | Adapt, in miniature |
| Creation idempotency | Removed in 2026 | Unique keys | Keep |
| Completion trust | Allowlisted relayers; refund after expiry | Relay solvers; escape hatch unknown | Ask Relay; keep a permissionless exit in §12a |
| Underpay UX | Once sent payers to email support, now silent | Minimum shown before send only | Avoid both |
| Client callbacks | No payload; server is the truth | `onCredited` | Copy the rule |

**Copy the conventions.** Daimo's final-state rule maps onto Settle Kit's compare-and-set transitions: if `credited` and `refunded` are declared final in `@settle-kit/core`, a late or repeated webhook can never pull a deposit backwards, which §6 already demands in prose. Daimo's split between `expectedUnits` and `receivedUnits` fits Settle Kit's `amount_in` and `amount_out`; recording the router's expected output at detection lets `accept: "any"` report variance instead of silently crediting a bad fill. Daimo's retry schedule answers the §13 delivery question directly. Its `check` call maps onto the send-attempt row: when the wallet returns a hash, ask the server to scan that one address immediately instead of waiting for a webhook. Its payload-free client callbacks express a rule worth writing down: widget callbacks are UI hints, and only the server's `onEvent` credits. And Daimo still polls in production, which supports leaving server-sent events for later.

**Adapt the single-use safety ideas to reusable addresses.** Daimo's one-hour addresses bound how long anyone must watch an address. Settle Kit's reusable addresses are friendlier for exchange address books and late deposits, but the reconciliation set grows with every address forever, which §13 already worries about; scanning by recent activity, with webhooks as the primary signal, keeps the budget flat. Refund routing needs the thinking Daimo did: because one Settle Kit address serves both the wallet and QR paths, its refund target cannot be the connected wallet, and refunding to the sender is unsafe when the sender is an exchange. Either point refunds at an app-controlled address and let the app return funds, or confirm how Relay chooses a refund target and when it falls back to the recovery address. Daimo's server-driven tree is overkill for two methods, but two of its habits are cheap: skip a picker with one option, and show unavailable routes disabled with a reason. Daimo also handles payments where source and destination match with a dedicated `sameChainFinish` step; Settle Kit's design never mentions a user who already holds USDC on Base, likely the most common deposit for a Base app, and it is unknown whether Relay issues a deposit address for that route. If Settle Kit ever adds fixed-amount checkout, Daimo's rule (an amount present means checkout) is the right API, but one third-party summary says Relay deposit addresses support only exact-input amounts, so checkout cannot ride on them without verification ([Relay deposit addresses](https://docs.relay.link/features/deposit-addresses)).

**Avoid Daimo's regressions.** The 2026 rebuild dropped creation idempotency, payload-carrying callbacks and the refunded event, pushing dedupe work back onto integrators; Settle Kit's unique keys are its selling point and should stay. The old email-support underpay screen is the anti-pattern for any post-send problem: once money has moved, the screen must say where it is and what happens next. Settle Kit's own §9 Result screen offers "retry" on failed, which conflicts with its no-second-send rule whenever the failure happened after funds left the wallet. For §12a, Daimo's trust history is the warning: moving from anyone-can-complete to allowlisted relayers made liveness depend on one company, and shipping a rewrite after the audit left the live code unaudited. Daimo's mismatch between file headers and repo license is an easy mistake for an open-source kit to avoid, and copying its Solidity into a permissively licensed kit would import GPL obligations.

## Conclusion

Daimo's history shows that routing is the easy part of deposits. Daimo buys it from CCTP, LayerZero, 0x and now Relay. The hard parts are liveness and localization. Daimo solved liveness by centralizing completion in its own relayers, and it is betting the company on localization through sales-gated fiat partners that an open-source kit cannot cheaply copy. That leaves Settle Kit a clear lane, developer-owned correctness, which is exactly where Daimo went backwards. The flip side is that Settle Kit's integrators inherit Relay's trust model the way Daimo's inherit Daimo's: whoever runs the solvers decides whether stuck money moves.

So the most important open question for the design is not about UX. It is whether a Relay deposit address has any escape hatch the payer or app can trigger. The §12a roadmap is worth pursuing for exactly this reason, beyond fees and control: owning the address lets Settle Kit write a permissionless refund into the contract, the one guarantee Daimo dropped.
